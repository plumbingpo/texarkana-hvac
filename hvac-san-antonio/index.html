When Your System Fails in San Antonio

The emergency calls we answer tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so warm indoor air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and call us. That small step can save you from a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in San Antonio

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A capacitor that's starting to fail costs little to replace, yet the same part, neglected for a season, can ruin the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or feels wrong in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
